## Course Information

### Course Description

This graduate seminar explores advanced topics in human factors and ergonomics through student-led presentations and discussions. Students will critically analyze current research, standards, and practices in human factors engineering, usability, and human-centered design. Topics may include cognitive ergonomics, human-computer interaction, safety and risk analysis, workload assessment, and domain-specific applications.

### Required Course Materials

**No textbook required.**

Course readings are available in the [readings directory](https://github.com/rogerlew/advanced-human-factors/tree/Spring2026/readings), which contains a catalog of articles, standards, and reference materials.

---

## Assessment

### Student Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Analyze** human factors research and apply findings to practical design problems
2. **Evaluate** products, systems, and interfaces using established usability and human factors methods
3. **Communicate** complex human factors concepts through presentations and written reports
4. **Synthesize** current literature and standards to inform human-centered design decisions
5. **Develop** domain specialization through self-exploration of human factors topics
6. **Demonstrate** awareness of industry practices through peer discussion

## Course Policies & Procedures

### Attendance Policy

As a graduate seminar, regular attendance and active participation are essential. You are responsible for your class attendance. Participation in discussions is designed to support your learning and build community.

**Remote/Asynchronous Students:**
Remote students may attend asynchronously by viewing class recordings. Recordings are kept on a short-term basis to provide a seminar experience for students who cannot attend synchronously. As an artifact of attendance, remote students are expected to maintain course notes documenting their engagement with the material. Course notes are due as an upload:
- Before midterm grades
- At the end of the semester

**Class Recordings:**
Students who miss a class may view the recordings remotely. **Do not share or publish course recordings.** Recordings are intended solely for enrolled students in this course.

**Excused Absences:**
In accordance with [UI academic procedures](https://www.uidaho.edu/registrar/faculty-staff/attendance-policy), reasonable opportunity to make up work will be provided when absences result from:
- Participation in official university activities
- Personal or family illness
- Other compelling circumstances

Please contact the instructor as soon as possible, ideally before the missed class, if you need to request an excused absence.

### Late Assignments

Late assignments are not accepted without an approved extension before the due date. If you anticipate difficulty meeting a deadline, request an extension before the due date with a brief explanation and proposed new submission date.

### Artificial Intelligence Policy

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools—including but not limited to ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude, Grammarly, and similar tools—may be used in this course if that use is properly documented and cited. When using AI assistance, clearly indicate what tools were used and how they contributed to your work.
